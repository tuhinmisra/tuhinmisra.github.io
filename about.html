About

An engineer who wants to see the design fly.

TM

I am a sophomore studying Aerospace Engineering at Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University in Daytona Beach, with an expected graduation in May 2029. My central interest is aircraft design, both the creation of new aircraft and the modification of existing ones for missions they were never originally built to perform.

That interest shows up in several parts of my work. As an undergraduate researcher in the Computational Fluids and Aerodynamics Laboratory, I use computational fluid dynamics to study how air behaves around a shape. As a co-founder and flight lead at Slingshot Aeronautics, I help turn designs into hardware and carry them through flight operations.

Outside of coursework, I pursue personal projects that stretch my understanding. One is a glider designed and flight tested for performance. Another is a conceptual study of what it would take for a small drone to reach Mach 1.

I believe an aircraft is best understood as an answer to a mission. When the mission changes, the answer must change with it, and that process of reasoning from purpose to geometry is what I enjoy most.

What I work with

Design

Conceptual and preliminary sizing, configuration selection, performance estimation, and stability analysis.

Analysis

Computational fluid dynamics, aerodynamic characterization, and trade studies that compare competing options fairly.

Testing

Building, flight testing, and learning from results so that each iteration improves on the last.
